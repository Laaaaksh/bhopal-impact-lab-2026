# Judging

The jury includes the organisers and a representative of the problem's real users: a department officer where possible, or a domain expert. Scoring leans toward **day-one impact**: what the user gets the week after the event.

## How the day ends

1. **17:30** Submissions close.
2. **17:35** Jury and mentors review every submission for about an hour and pick who presents. Not every team presents.
3. **18:30** Each chosen team gets **6 minutes** to present and demo, then **4 minutes** of jury questions.
4. **19:30** Winners announced.

## Scoring card

One card per judge per presenting team. Each criterion is scored 0 to 5. **Day-one impact counts double**, so the maximum is 25.

| Criterion | What is scored | Scoring guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Day-one impact (x2)** | Is it ready to use, with real impact, on day one? | 5 ready, high impact · 4 almost ready, high impact · 3 ready, mild impact · 2 almost ready, mild impact · 1 not ready or not impactful |
| **Product** | User experience, ease of use, onboarding | 5 polished, works first try · 4 well designed, fits the workflow · 3 usable with little learning · 2 functional, needs learning · 1 hard to use |
| **Idea** | How creative and impactful the idea is, whatever the result | 5 rare insight · 4 creative and original · 3 solid but predictable · 2 close to what exists · 1 already exists |
| **Clarity of demo** | Are the problem, the user and the outcome obvious? | 5 impeccable pitch and demo · 4 good pitch, convincing demo · 3 ok pitch, demo worked · 2 weak narrative · 1 no narrative or demo failed |

## What the jury looks for in each track

**Track 1, Bhumi Vikas Sahayak**

- Every answer shows its rule number and the text it came from.
- The tool says "not found" when the rules are silent, and never invents a rule.
- Numbers from tables (fees, setbacks) are right.
- The team shows how often the tool is right on the sample questions.

**Track 2, Visarjan Ghat Watch**

- The ranking of ghats is traceable to the readings and the CPCB limits.
- Missing data shows as missing, never as clean.
- The brief is short and readable by a BMC officer in Hindi and English.
- The team is honest about what the data does not cover.

## Tie-break

If two teams tie, the higher day-one impact score wins.
